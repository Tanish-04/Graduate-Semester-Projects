US Foreign Aid Visualization: Project Report

1. Dataset Description

The dataset, US_FOREIGN_BUDGET_COMPLETE, contains detailed records of foreign aid provided by the United States to countries around the world. The dataset is available at this link: US Foreign Assistance. I downloaded it from here and used it for this project. Each row represents a funding record for a specific country, year, and category. The main columns are:

  • Country Name: The recipient country of the aid.
  • Region Name: The geographical region of the country.
  • Income Group Name: The World Bank income classification of the country (e.g., Low Income, Upper Middle Income).
  • Fiscal Year: The year the aid was allocated.
  • current_amount: The amount of aid (in USD) provided.
  • US Category Name: The sector or purpose of the aid (e.g., Health, Economic Development).

The dataset is cleaned to include only valid, non-zero funding records with specified countries and income groups.

2. Questions the Visualization is Designed to Answer

  • How is US foreign aid distributed across different regions and countries?
  • What are the main sectors (categories) for which aid is provided?
  • How does aid distribution vary by country income group?
  • What is the flow of funding from sectors to regions and countries?

3. Visualization Design and Evolution

Visualizations Created

  • World Map: Shows the distribution of aid by country and region.
  • Treemap: Displays funding by country, color-coded by income group.
  • Sankey Diagram: Illustrates the flow of funding from sectors to regions and countries.

Design Evolution

  • Initial Design: Started with a simple map and bar chart to show country-level funding.
  • Improvements: Replaced the bar chart with a treemap for better use of space and to encode income group via color. Added a Sankey diagram to visualize the flow of funds from sectors to regions and countries, providing a more holistic view.
  • Interactivity: Added filtering by year, search functionality, and interactive highlighting to allow users to focus on specific countries or categories.

Marks and Channels

  • World Map: Uses geographic regions (marks: country shapes), color (channel: region), and tooltips for details.
  • Treemap: Uses rectangles sized by funding amount (mark: area), color-coded by income group (channel: color).
  • Sankey Diagram: Uses nodes (marks: rectangles) and links (marks: paths) to show flow, with width encoding funding amount (channel: size).
  • Other Channels: Tooltips, hover highlights, and clickable elements for interaction.

Techniques Used

  • D3.js: For all data-driven visualizations and DOM manipulation.
  • TopoJSON: For efficient map rendering.
  • Data Filtering and Aggregation: For year selection, country/category grouping, and income group classification.
  • Color Palettes: Carefully chosen for accessibility and clarity.
  • Responsive Design: Layout adapts to different screen sizes.

4. How the Visualization Answers the Questions

  • Aid Distribution by Region/Country: The world map provides an immediate visual overview, with color and tooltips revealing regional and country-level details.
  • Main Sectors for Aid: The Sankey diagram's leftmost nodes represent sectors, showing which categories receive the most funding and how funds flow to regions and countries.
  • Variation by Income Group: The treemap encodes income group by color, making it easy to compare funding across different economic classifications.
  • Funding Flow: The Sankey diagram visually traces the path of funds from sector to region to country, making complex relationships easy to follow.
  • Temporal Analysis: The year filter allows users to see how patterns change over time, supporting trend analysis.
  • Search and Focus: The search bar and interactive highlights let users quickly find and focus on specific countries or categories, supporting both overview and detail-on-demand.
